Day 2 — Exploratory Analysis & Visualisation

Tables and charts that survive a reviewer

Where the day goes

Summary statistics are easy; defensible summary statistics presented well are not. Today is about the gap between the two.

Sessions

# Session Material
1 Publication tables with gt and gtsummary scripts/gt-summary-tables.R, scripts/gt-tables-basics.R
2 ggplot2 from the grammar up scripts/nhanes-ggplot-basics.R
3 Live indicator data: WDI and OWID scripts/wdi-indicators.R, scripts/owid-tutorial.R
4 Chart critique and redesign scripts/gt-bar-defence.R

Scripts in this folder

  • 10-useful-commands.R — the dplyr verbs you will reach for every day
  • birthweight-pregnancy.R — a worked EDA on health data
  • nhanes-ggplot-advanced.R — facets, scales, annotation, themes

A note on API sessions

wdi-indicators.R and owid-tutorial.R hit live APIs. If the room’s connection is unreliable, run them before the session and cache the result; the OWID script includes fallback approaches for when the primary endpoint is down.

What you should be able to do by 16:30

  • produce a formatted table you would put in a paper, from raw data, in under ten lines
  • choose a chart type by asking what comparison the reader must make
  • pull an indicator series for any country from the World Bank without leaving R
  • say what is wrong with a bad chart in specific terms
